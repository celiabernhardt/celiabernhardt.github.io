Haredi schools make up outsized portion of cafeteria health code violations

A CUNY Newmark analysis across Brooklyn shows that Haredi school cafeterias were nearly twice as likely to have critical food safety violations than their counterparts.



CELIA BERNHARDT


Students at Hasidic and Haredi schools in Brooklyn may be more at risk of food poisoning in their cafeterias than their counterparts across the borough, an analysis of Department of Health and Mental Hygiene data shows. If a rate of nearly one (0.97) critical health violation per school in Brooklyn across calendar years 2024 and 2025 wasn’t already a cause for concern, Haredi schools have a violation rate nearly twice as high at 1.79.

One education policy expert said the numbers tracked with Haredi institutions’ approach to managing schools in general.

“It's not at all surprising that they're not paying attention to health code regulations and that there'd be a lot of violations,” he said. “It’s a school system that has been built intentionally to live outside of… as they see it, to live outside of government oversight. So I would imagine that looking to see what health code rule they need to follow is really low on a lot of the school leaders' lists as far as priorities, what they do on a daily basis.”



Haredi schools, especially boys’ Yeshivas, are notorious for low compliance with New York’s curriculum regulations – they devote significant instructional time to theological studies and less time on math, English, science and the like. Florida Atlantic University Sociology Professor Matty Lichtenstein describes them as “rooted in educational logics that differ fundamentally from those of state educational agencies” in her research.

New York City’s DOHMH inspects all school cafeterias across the city at least once per year, recording any violations they see and categorizing them by severity. Newmark looked at critical health violations in particular – those include a range of problems like pests, food handlers wearing jewelry on their arms or hands, cafeteria managers lacking a Food Protection Certification, and more.

Haredi schools made up about 25% of all Brooklyn schools inspected from 2024 to 2025, but accounted for 46.6% of critical violations in that time. Non-Haredi private schools, by contrast, made up 16% of all schools inspected in those years and accounted for only 13.05% of critical violations across Brooklyn.

The worst offender in 2024 was Bnos Spinka, a boy’s school in Williamsburg, with 12 critical violations. In 2025 it was Yeshivas Novominsk, located in Borough Park.
